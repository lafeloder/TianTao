### Working Papers in Banking

#### 1. Soft Information Imbalance Is Bad for Fair Credit Allocation

*Job Market Paper*  
with Cal Muckley and Xiangyang Zhao

Examines how differences in lenders' reliance on soft information affect racial disparities in mortgage credit allocation.

**Presentations:** 2026 EFA Poster Session; 2026 EEA-ESEM; 12th Workshop on Banking and Institutions (Helsinki); IRMC 2026 Conference; 2026 EFiC Conference in Banking and Corporate Finance; 2026 IAFA Annual Conference; 2026 EDHEC Business School Research Seminar; 2026 UCD Financial Markets Research Seminar; 2026 IAF Conference; 2025 China Finance Annual Meeting; 2025 Irish Annual Doctoral Colloquium in Finance; 2025 Renmin University of China Research Seminar; 2025 UCD PhD Symposium.

#### 2. Loan Officers, Same-Sex Couple Mortgages and Non-discrimination Law

with Cal Muckley

Studies mortgage lending to same-sex couples and the role of non-discrimination laws in credit allocation.

**Presentations:** 2025 Paris December Finance Meeting; IFABS 2025 Oxford Conference; 2025 IAF Cork PhD Conference; 2024 UCD PhD Symposium.

#### 3. The Economic Costs of Bank Misconduct: Evidence from Newspaper Closures

with Kevin Gam, Cal Muckley and Xiang Yao

Examines the economic consequences of bank misconduct using newspaper closures as a source of variation in local monitoring.

**Presentations:** The 18th CEA (Europe) & 37th CEA (UK) 2026 Annual Conference; 2026 EFiC Corporate Governance and Misconduct Workshop; 2026 World Finance Conference; 2026 EFiC Conference in Banking and Corporate Finance; 2026 Scottish Doctoral Colloquium; 2026 UCD PhD Symposium; 2026 IAF Conference.

#### 4. An Expensive ‘Blind Spot’ in Fintech Lending: Soft Information Decided Loans

with Cal Muckley

Studies the role of soft information in fintech lending and the consequences of lending decisions that are difficult to capture using standardized hard information.

#### 5. AI Is Not All You Need: Generative AI and the Quality of Financial Services

with Haoran Wu and Jiaquan Yao

Examines how generative AI affects the quality of financial services.
